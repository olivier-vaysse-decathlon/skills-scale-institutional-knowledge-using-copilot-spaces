# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

OctoAcme manages cross-functional projects through five connected stages: initiation, planning, iterative execution and tracking, release and deployment, and closeout through retrospectives and continuous improvement. Initiation establishes the problem, measurable outcomes, stakeholders, timeline, and initial risks. Planning turns approved work into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, dependencies, milestones, and a Definition of Done.

The process assigns clear ownership across its core roles. The Project Manager coordinates delivery, schedules, risks, dependencies, meetings, and stakeholder communication. The Product Manager defines outcomes and success metrics, prioritizes the backlog and roadmap, and makes trade-offs based on customer and business value. Developers implement and test the solution, while QA validates quality and acceptance criteria and stakeholders provide inputs and approvals.

Delivery relies on small, testable increments, visible project tracking, regular communication, and evidence-based decisions. Teams use a project board and short standups to expose progress and blockers, hold weekly delivery and PM–PdM syncs, demo completed work, and maintain regular stakeholder updates. Risks and dependencies are recorded in a shared register and escalated as they grow in impact or urgency.

Quality and reliability are embedded throughout delivery: CI runs unit and integration tests, linting, security scans, and smoke tests; pull requests remain small and include issue and acceptance-criteria context; and work requires review before merge. Releases require completed acceptance criteria, passing checks, release notes, rollback plans, and post-deployment verification. Retrospectives follow sprints, releases, milestones, and incidents, producing small sets of owned, dated improvement actions that the team measures and follows up on.
